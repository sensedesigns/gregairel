The Climb

Ten camps. Two ways up. One doll waiting on its second eye.

No shortcuts on the mountain or in the office. The hard way is usually the right way.
On earning it

This is a climb, and you are already at the trailhead.

Ten camps stand between here and the top, each one higher and thinner than the last. Wu-Tang is on the radio the whole way up, which is less of a joke than it sounds: the Clan took its mythology from Shaolin, Shaolin's founding legend is Bodhidharma, and Bodhidharma is Daruma. He sat facing a wall for nine years. The Clan came nine deep. This site counts nine entities under one canopy. Make of that what you like.

A Daruma doll is sold with both eyes blank. You ink the left when you set a goal and the right only when you reach it. Pick a track below and the left eye opens. The right one is up to you.

03 Rules of the mountain

How this
actually works

  • Hints are free, and they cost you nothing but pride. Each camp offers three, released a few minutes apart. Using them is recorded on your certificate and does not disqualify you from anything.
  • There is an exit. Any camp will hand you the answer outright if you ask for it. Take it and you drop off the leaderboard, but you keep climbing and you still finish.
  • Rest between camps. Climb too fast and the altitude catches you — the mountain will hold you at each camp for a short spell before it lets you go on. Read the page while you wait. It is usually where the next clue is.
  • The prize is a draw, not a race. Everyone who reaches the summit within 48 hours of starting goes into a random draw for the reward. Finishing first wins you a place on the leaderboard and nothing else. There is no cap on how many people can finish.
  • The leaderboard is optional. You can finish, claim a reward, and stay off it entirely.

One climber, one run. Automating it gains you nothing — the draw is random and the gates are fixed.
