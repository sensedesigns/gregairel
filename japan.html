The idea

The other guides on this site were written after the boots came off. This one is different — it's the plan, published while it's still a plan. Ten nights on the ground in late March, routed so the trip rides the cherry blossom front south: Tokyo as it peaks, Fuji from a lakeside ryokan, then Kyoto just as its trees catch up.

The full write-up — what actually happened, what I'd change, the photos — lands here after the trip. Until then, consider this a working document for anyone sketching a similar route.

When
Late March – early April 2027, 10 nights on the ground
Route
Tokyo (3) → Kawaguchiko (2) → Kyoto (4, with Nara and Osaka day trips) → Tokyo (1)

Bases
Shibuya (station-adjacent), a lakefront ryokan on Kawaguchiko's north shore, and quiet Gion in Kyoto
Gateway

Day by day — the plan

Day 1 — Land → Shibuya

Land mid-afternoon; immigration runs 45–60 minutes (have the Visit Japan Web QR ready before you board). Narita Express direct to Shibuya, hotel by early evening. Cross the Scramble once, watch it once from above, bowl of ramen, lights out by 10 — arriving in the afternoon means you want to be tired-ish by 9 PM local.

Day 2 — Meiji + record digging

Jet lag wakes you at 5 — use it. Meiji Jingu at 8 AM, walk out through Harajuku and Omotesando. At noon, the Udagawacho dig: Manhattan Records (opens 12), Disk Union, Tower Records' nine floors. These are the blocks where Nujabes ran T Records and Guinness Records. Night: JBS on Dogenzaka — a vinyl-only jazz and soul bar, about fifteen seats, cash only.

Day 3 — Old Tokyo + Shinjuku night

Senso-ji by 7:30 AM, before the tour buses, with Nakamise street snacks on the way out. Midday in Ueno Park — Tokyo should be at or near peak bloom. Night: yakitori in Omoide Yokocho, then a Golden Gai bar crawl.

Day 4 — To Kawaguchiko

Morning move: have the hotel takkyubin the big bag straight to Kyoto (~¥2,500, arrives next day) — the Fuji leg is daypack only, and this is the single best logistics trick in Japan. Fuji Excursion limited express from Shinjuku to Kawaguchiko (~2 hrs, ~¥4,130, reserved seats). Check in mid-afternoon, sunset from the north shore, onsen, kaiseki dinner.

Day 5 — Fuji day

Chureito Pagoda at 7 AM — 398 steps up to the snow-capped postcard shot; mornings are clearest. Afternoon: hike Mt. Tenjo or Panoramadai for the lake-and-Fuji views — the March substitute for a summit that's closed until July. Onsen, round two.

Day 6 — To Kyoto + Gion at dusk

Bus from Kawaguchiko to Mishima Station (~90 min, ~¥2,600), then Shinkansen to Kyoto (~2 hrs) — no Tokyo backtrack. Check in, then Gion as the lanterns come on: Hanamikoji, dinner on Pontocho alley.

Day 7 — Kyoto east

Fushimi Inari at 7 AM — hike past the first plaza; the upper mountain loop is where it empties out. Lunch graze through Nishiki Market. 2 PM: Kiyomizu-dera, then walk down Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka to Yasaka Shrine and Maruyama Park hanami.

Day 8 — Kyoto west + Miyako Odori

Arashiyama bamboo grove before 8 AM, when it's silent and empty, with Tenryu-ji next door. 1:30 PM: Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion. Evening: the Miyako Odori — the geisha spring dances at Gion Kobu Kaburenjo, which open April 1st. Tickets go on sale in winter; set a reminder.

Day 9 — Nara → Osaka night

Kintetsu line from Kyoto to Nara (~40 min): Todai-ji's Great Buddha at 10, the bowing deer, the lantern paths of Kasuga Taisha, lunch in Naramachi. Late afternoon, Kintetsu straight to Osaka-Namba (~40 min, ~¥700) — you surface five minutes from Dotonbori. The Glico sign at dusk, a takoyaki and kushikatsu crawl, then duck into Hozenji Yokocho one block south — a lantern-lit stone alley with a moss-covered Fudō statue. Evening train back to Kyoto (~50 minutes all-in via Umeda).

Day 10 — Sakura walk → back to Tokyo

9 AM: Ginkaku-ji and the Philosopher's Path — by this date, likely peak bloom. Early afternoon Nozomi Shinkansen back to Tokyo (~2¼ hrs, ~¥14,000), check back into Shibuya. Last night: Shibuya Sky at sunset, or one more listening bar.

Day 11 — Home

Morning vinyl and souvenir run (Tower opens at 10), then the Narita Express with a comfortable three-hour buffer before the flight. Cross the date line the good way this time — land the same day you left.

Logistics notes

  • Rail: Skip the JR Pass — point-to-point on this route totals roughly ¥30–35K and beats it. A Suica in Apple Wallet covers every local leg, including the Osaka loop.
  • Cash: Temples, the listening bars, and small izakaya are cash-only. 7-Eleven ATMs take US cards.
  • Weather: Tokyo and Kyoto run 45–60°F — layers. Kawaguchiko sits at elevation and drops near freezing at night.
  • Bloom watch: The JMA and Weathernews forecasts firm up about six weeks out. Tokyo typically peaks late March, Kyoto early April — the route follows the front either way.
  • Fuji note: The mountain itself is closed and snowbound in March — climbing season is July through early September. Chureito and Mt. Tenjo are the play on this trip; the summit is a future guys' trek.
  • Luggage: Takkyubin (hotel-to-hotel luggage forwarding) is cheap, near-universal, and turns multi-city Japan into a daypack trip. Use it for every city change.
