Bio

Entrepreneur, technology leader, logistics innovator. Fifteen years in software, then a hard left into e-commerce and freight.

01 Biography

From the codebase
to the warehouse floor

A software engineer and full-stack developer by trade, with over 15 years in software design, systems architecture, and resource management.

Greg graduated from Baldwin Wallace University in 2010 with a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science Web Applications Engineering and a minor in Studio Art. He spent the first decade of his career in high-level technical roles before making a bold transition into e-commerce and logistics.

The entrepreneurial journey began in earnest in 2017 with the launch of Sense Designs, a technology company focused on helping small businesses get online. It started with building small websites for friends and family, but it introduced him to a new inner drive to create something bigger.

In the summer of 2018, Greg was approached with the idea for My College Crate. After many long nights and weekends, the brand launched that fall — in true side-hustle fashion, worked out of a McDonald's parking lot during a lunch break from his day job.

The initial launch produced zero sales. Realising a pivot was needed, Greg steered the company to ten straight quarters of revenue growth and yearly profitability, and developed three more brands: The Care Crate Co., Happy Pet Crate, and My Hero Crate. When fulfillment partners couldn't keep up with the exponential growth, he brought all production in-house — going from a software engineer in a cushy office to building care packages in a 1,000 sq ft warehouse with his best friend and a stopwatch, eventually optimising the process enough for the business to scale efficiently.

When the COVID-19 pandemic hit, Greg pivoted again, first creating pandemic survival kits and then transitioning to a B2B model that revived the business. Recognising the struggle other small businesses faced with shipping, he launched Shipping Pilot in 2020. Based in the historic Darice building in Strongsville, the company scaled to $16MM in revenue, fulfilled over 750,000 orders, and served customers in more than 50 countries.

After selling Shipping Pilot to a fellow Strongsville entrepreneur in 2023 and leading the transition through 2025, Greg joined Fulfill.com as VP of Sales & Business Development. Today he leverages his background as both a brand owner and a 3PL founder to help logistics providers nationwide scale through superior technology and operational playbooks.

02 Beginnings

Before any of it
was a business

The first website I built was on Angelfire, in grade school.

There were a few. A wrestling site. A Pokémon site. One for Final Fantasy. Then the magnum opus — G-Funk's Realm, which loaded with a MIDI of Biggie's "Big Poppa" and held its layout together with iframes nested several layers deep. It was, by any standard, terrible. I learned more building it than in any class I sat through that decade.

Thirty years later I'm still building websites with music embedded in them, for no reason beyond wanting to. Draw your own conclusions.

The next step was asking for Visual Basic for Dummies as a Christmas present. Not to learn programming in any structured way — to write progz and punters for AOL.

Progz were homemade utilities traded around the AOL underground: chat-room tools, mass-mailers, ASCII art bots. Punters were the less polite cousin — programs that exploited holes in the AOL client to knock somebody else offline.

My AIM handle was troublesome g. That was not a mistake.

The calculator trade

Every kid at my high school was required to carry a TI-83 for math class — a hundred-dollar device that became worthless to its owner the day the course ended. So I bought them. Twenty-five dollars, cash, most of the time. Then they went on eBay.

At one point I supplied an entire classroom in Oklahoma with TI-83s and got back the nicest thank-you note I have ever received. Apologies to the parents. Full credit to my mom, who shipped every single one of them.

One of the few bits of feedback still standing, twenty-odd years later.

Sense

After a short and unremarkable career in graffiti, I kept the handle — sense — and put it on a business. The first site I built under that name I built for fun, and for nothing else. Then it became websites for other people, and then apps.

The Funwall, Cleveland, somewhere around 2002. The handle outlasted the spray cans.

I wrote and released the first bookmark manager on the Android Play Store — a home-screen widget, built for Android 2.0. Google eventually shipped their own. I have no evidence whatsoever that the two facts are connected, and I intend to keep believing they are.

Screenshots of the Angelfire era are still being excavated.
